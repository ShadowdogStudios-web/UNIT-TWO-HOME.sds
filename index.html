About this unit

What Unit Two is about

Unit Two follows the United States across its most consequential half-century — from a young republic pushing west and arguing over who it was for, to a country that finally splits over slavery, fights the Civil War, and struggles to rebuild a fairer nation during Reconstruction.

Rather than march through a textbook, each lesson drops students into a real decision, document, or dispute and asks them to work like a historian: read a source closely, weigh competing perspectives, and trace cause and effect. Many lessons use an Illinois and Midwestern lens — Fort Dearborn, the Black Hawk War, Nauvoo, the Lincoln–Douglas debates — so the national story stays close to home.

The unit comes in two halves. Part One builds the pressures of expansion and conflict; Part Two follows the country as the compromises collapse into war and its aftermath.

How students work

The historian’s toolkit

Read the source
Close reading of documents, images, maps, and songs — not just a textbook summary.
Weigh perspectives
The same event from more than one side — American and Mexican, North and South.
Trace cause & effect
How one decision set up the next — and how compromise gave way to war.
Make the argument
Use the evidence to take and defend a position, like a historian would.
